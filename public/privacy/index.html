Scrobbler for Sonos

Privacy

This service moves a play from your speaker to your own Last.fm and ListenBrainz accounts. It is built so that it cannot become a second copy of your listening history.

What is never stored

No listening history. There is no table of what you played. That is the schema rather than a policy: there is no scrobbles, history or plays table, and the migration that creates the database says so in a comment. A track exists in this service only while it is playing and until Last.fm or ListenBrainz accepts it. Then it is gone.

Logs do not contain track, artist or album names either. The logger drops any field with one of those names as a backstop, so a future mistake at a call site cannot turn a log into the record this service promises not to keep.

What is stored

  • Your Sonos authorization, encrypted with AES-GCM. It is what lets the service receive playback events. It is never used to control your speakers.
  • Your Last.fm session key and ListenBrainz token, encrypted the same way, plus the username each service reports back so the page can show you which account is connected.
  • Your household, group and room names, which are your own labels for your hardware, and which speakers are currently grouped together. The status page is unusable without them, and the room switches on the front page need them to work out which speakers an event belongs to.
  • Subscription bookkeeping and four timestamps: when a subscription renews, when the last Sonos event arrived, when the last scrobble was submitted, and when your account was created. Timestamps only, and no titles.
  • A login session: your browser holds a random token, and the database holds only its HMAC. A copy of the database cannot be used to sign in as you.
  • A hashed dedupe key per queued play, so the same track is not submitted twice. It is an HMAC, not a title, and it is discarded with the play.
  • Your own choices: which rooms may scrobble, and whether to accept radio, playback cast from an app, and tracks longer than twenty minutes. Three switches, plus one flag per speaker. A room switched off stays switched off even if that speaker leaves the network and comes back.

Who it is shared with

Only the services you connected. Plays go to Last.fm and ListenBrainz because that is what you asked for. Nothing is sold, and no page here loads analytics, advertising or any third-party script. The content security policy this site sends forbids loading one. The two typefaces are served from this domain rather than from a font CDN, because a webfont request carries your IP address and the page you are reading to whoever serves it.

Deleting everything

The Delete everything button on the front page unsubscribes from Sonos, wipes the durable storage holding anything in flight, and deletes your row, which every other table cascades from. It is immediate and there is no soft-delete copy. Revoking this integration in your Sonos account stops new events regardless.

Contact

Questions about your data go to hello@suvir.net. Anything that could expose somebody's credentials should go there too rather than to a public issue. The colophon has the security policy.

Every claim on this page is checkable: the service is open source under the MIT licence, and the schema that has no history table in it is in the repository.
